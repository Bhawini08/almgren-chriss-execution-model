# Methodology

## Objective

The model solves a finite-horizon liquidation problem that balances expected market-impact cost against uncertainty from carrying inventory while prices move.

## Discrete-time implementation

The execution horizon is divided into equal intervals. The optimal inventory path is generated from the discrete-time Almgren-Chriss urgency parameter, using the effective temporary-impact coefficient after accounting for permanent impact.

For zero risk aversion, the optimal schedule converges to uniform liquidation. As risk aversion increases, the trajectory becomes more front-loaded.

## Expected execution cost

Expected cost is decomposed into:

- temporary market impact
- permanent market impact
- spread cost

The model also converts expected cost into basis points of initial notional so results are economically interpretable.

## Execution risk

Cost variance is generated by price uncertainty applied to the remaining inventory through time. Holding more inventory for longer increases execution risk.

## Efficient frontier

The efficient frontier varies risk aversion across several orders of magnitude and records expected cost, cost standard deviation, objective value, and the fraction of inventory executed in the first half of the horizon.

This makes the cost-risk trade-off explicit rather than hiding it inside a single optimized schedule.

## TWAP benchmark

The optimized schedule is compared directly with TWAP. Under very low risk aversion, the two should be nearly identical. As risk aversion rises, the Almgren-Chriss schedule should achieve a lower mean-variance objective by trading earlier.

## Monte Carlo validation

Implementation shortfall is simulated by adding Gaussian price shocks to the analytical impact cost. The simulation is not used to define the solution. It is used to verify that the empirical mean and standard deviation of shortfall converge toward the analytical moments.

For the default parameter set, simulated mean and standard deviation closely match the analytical values.

## Parameter sensitivity

The analysis varies:

- risk aversion
- volatility
- temporary-impact coefficient

and records expected cost, shortfall volatility, and early-execution fraction.

## Limitations

The current parameter set is stylized rather than estimated from proprietary intraday data. Impact is modeled linearly and volatility is assumed constant over the execution horizon. These simplifications are appropriate for demonstrating the framework but should not be confused with production calibration for a specific asset or venue.
